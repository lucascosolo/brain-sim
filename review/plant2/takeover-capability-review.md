# Independent takeover capability review

Scope: completed R1 and E6 reports, E6's results-review scope statement, and the final R2 contract identified as2454f06. This reviews capability and direction, not code correctness, numerical reconstruction, or another reviewer's work. R2 results were not available at this review.

**Progress is real but narrower than an accepted memory capability.** R1 demonstrates that choosing feedback writers by observed continuation activity can improve settled reconstruction beyond merely reducing writer count. At duty/1000, R3 content reaches.995 versus.205 inherited and.710 count-matched random on the same raster. That supports writer identity as useful information. It does not establish a live R3 system: the favorable result uses a settled copy, and R3 mainly recovers the experimenter's assigned plateau key.

E6 demonstrates a new causal use of sensory history during encoding. On seed101 at canonical1000, joint index rises.440→.865, with more own-cell responses and fewer spurious responses; the exact flip-count control does not reproduce the gain. Calling this simple silence would ignore the measurements. Calling it reliable selective memory would also overreach: the frozen screen fails both overall and oldest specificity, and held-out seeds remain unrun. The one-cue margins neither erase the large measured improvement nor justify accepting the rule. Independent recall losses within tolerance and mixed F40 transfer remain costs, not evidence of unchanged performance.

**The central missing capability remains usable content access under interference.** E6's canonical1000 inherited content is only.065; assigned-A feedback reaches.425. R1's favorable feedback result and E6's favorable index result cannot be added into an unmeasured combined system. Nor does the assigned-A failure prove that every possible activity-based writer must fail: that store is not an upper bound. The defensible conclusion is that neither tested intervention supplies complete canonical recall-and-reconstruction at the frozen deadlines.

The experimenter still supplies sparse sensory coordinates and values, discrete encoding episodes, random plateau assemblies, quiet learning, prescribed partial cues, and settle/test scheduling. The plant has not learned when to store, how to allocate its own keys, or when to retrieve. Independent novel-cue rejection is not understanding unseen related experiences. Capacity growth, graceful forgetting, robust online access, and autonomous representation remain unestablished. Hence no Stage1 completion or autonomy claim follows, and no Stage2 escalation is warranted.

**R2 should choose the next hypothesis, not manufacture progress.** It changes the input distribution while preserving blockwise line counts; even a null PASS is an explanation result, not a new ability. Its changed fine timing, cue composition, eligibility, coin alignment, and neural state prevent an additive frequency-versus-correlation attribution.

If R2 produces replicated material structure sensitivity without the declared recall loss, the strongest next direction is a separately contracted **activity-driven pattern-separation mechanism before storage**, using observed coactivation and learned competition rather than family labels or supplied prototypes. Its purpose would be to learn a less-confusable representation from the original F10 inputs; it must preserve old memories and demonstrate joint recall, not merely suppress shared features. This is a new mechanistic hypothesis, not a new pseudocount or threshold for E6. If R2 instead preserves failure, or is mixed, defer that mechanism choice and narrow the load/operating-state explanation first. Neither outcome licenses retuning the rejected E6 rule or silently deploying R3.
