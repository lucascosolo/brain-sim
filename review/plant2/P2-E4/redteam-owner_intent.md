# P2-E4 draft red-team: owner intent lens

Fresh reviewer given only the files (draft at commit 953851e); each finding then checked by a separate refuter. Run 2026-10-10. Recorded verbatim, before synthesis.

## Verdict

The draft cannot be frozen as written. Its never-probed cohort rule reserves every item from 1 to 120, so the M = 250 gated block cannot be scheduled. With that fixed and the majors addressed, it can be frozen. It keeps the owner's main lines: the stress tests stay ungated, parameters stay frozen, the gated numbers come from a live rec, and it adds no mechanism. Three things matter most. (1) O4/O5 are gated on copies where learning is paused, after a 10 s rest, so they are not online; in exploration, the rest changes the recovery outcome, in a direction that depends on load. (2) Exploration says the likeliest verdict is ONLINE FAIL on memory-assembly (index) recall at M = 1,000 while content recall holds. The verdict labels, predictions and power design should expect this, and a Decision section must limit what PASS means and keep the owner's order (the structured-input diagnostic, P2-E5, comes next whatever the result). (3) The 'no write oracle' arm is mislabelled, and the transfer and re-exposure arms widen the scope and should be cut. At about 50 min per seed, the experiment is worth its cost once trimmed.

## Findings

### FID-1 [blocker] The never-probed cohort rule makes the M = 250 gated block (and early rolling slots) impossible to schedule

- where: docs/plant2/P2-E4-online-memory.draft.md lines 127-146 (gated blocks, never-probed cohorts) and 115-125 (rolling slots)
- evidence: The pool is items 1-120, and all of them go into three reserved cohorts of 40 (lines 142-143). A reserved item receives 'no probe of any kind' before its cohort slot (lines 144-145). The M = 250 block is steps 11-250 (line 127). Until step 121 every stored item is reserved, so recent (ages 1-20), uniform, full and blank-target slots have no legal target. Ages 1-20 are all unreserved only from step 141 on. Steps 11-121 hold 111 slots, but the block has only 60 novel slots, and its 40 cohort slots must fall in steps 151-250. So at least 51 slots cannot be filled. Rolling steps 1-10 have the same problem. The phrase 'or on 1..k-1 early on' (line 132) shows the conflict with the pool was not noticed.
- recommendation: Re-specify before freezing, keeping the owner's never-probed cohort of the oldest items. For example, start the first gated block after the pool (steps 141-250, counts re-derived by the power check), or move the first gated load so its block begins after step 140. State what rolling slots hold while no unreserved item exists (novel, or blank with no target). Add a schedule-builder unit test that asserts every slot constraint at M = 250, 500 and 1,000.
- independent verifier: **confirmed** (severity should be blocker): I re-derived the schedule from the draft text. The pool is items 1-120, split into 3 disjoint cohorts of 40 (lines 142-143), so every pool item is reserved. Reserved items get no probe of any kind, including full cues and blank targets (144-145). ### FID-2 [major] Habituation and recovery (O4, O5) are gated on copies with learning paused and after a 10 s rest, not online; the rest shifts the outcome, in a direction that depends on load

- where: Draft lines 160-173 (habituation arm), 76 ('no quiet() and no settle anywhere'), 38-41 (hypothesis: 'while it learns ... keeps recall through 30 s ... recovers within 10 s'); DECISIONS.md rulings (online experiment 'tests recall while the system keeps learning ... covers ... repeated-cue habituation'; P2-E4 keeps 'the continuous timeline ... the no-settle rule'); review/ledger.jsonl row 91 ('recovery as a paired change') against O5's absolute bar
- evidence: Copies are taken 'with no settle, no learning'. The recovery cue follows 10 s of pure background (one tau), and the control copy gets 39.7 s of background (about 4 tau, close to settled). EXPLORATORY (habituation_recovery.py; copies taken from an online line with blank slots). Seed 42, M = 500, 10 items: recovery passed both criteria in 10/10 after the draft's 10 s rest (mean recall 0.97), but in only 7/10 when the same 10 s was spent learning 20 new items (0.88). A control with no repetition and the same learning passed 10/10 (0.94). Seed 43, M = 1,000, 7 scored items, the reverse: after the rest 5/7 (up to 41 intrusions), with continued learning 7/7 (at most 4). The draft-style control after 39.7 s of background passed 4/7 (up to 144 intrusions). So O5 and its retest baseline sample a rested operating point, not the online one, and the sign of the bias flips with load.
- recommendation: Keep learning running on every habituation copy. Interleave the repetition and the 10 s recovery with ordinary encodings: for example, the repeated half cue in the probe slot of 100 consecutive steps, with recovery as 20 ordinary steps. Make the control copy identical except for the repetition. Gate O5 against that control as the ledger records, or say plainly that it is absolute. If learning must pause, label O4/O5 'learning paused, 10 s rest', and remove 'no settle anywhere' and the 'while it learns' wording from the hypothesis.
- independent verifier: **partly** (severity should be major): The structural part holds. Copies are taken 'with no settle, no learning' (line 162). Recovery follows 10 s of background (166), and the control has 39.7 s of background, about 4 tau (170). That contradicts line 76 ('no settle anywhere') and the owner's description of the experiment, which 'tests recall while the system keeps learning ... covers ... repeated-cue habituation' (DECISIONS.md 337-339). Ledger row 91 says the draft adopted 'recovery as a paired change', but O5 (line 186) is an absolute bar. I re-derived the exploratory numbers from hab_s42_M500.json and hab_s43_M1000.json. Seed 42, M = 500: A 10/10 (mean recall 0.97), B 7/10 (0.88), C 10/10, D 10/10. Seed 43, M = 1,000, 7 scored items: A 5/7 (max 41 intrusions), B 7/7 (max 4), C 4/7 (max 144), D 7/7. The overstated part: 'the direction depends on load' rests on one seed per load, so seed and load are confounded. The samples are small: 3 and 2 discordant pairs, sign-test p = 0.25 and 0.5. At M = 500 the content outcomes (missing lines, intrusions) were identical in all four arms, so the difference there is memory recall only. Whether habituation has to interleave learning is also open to interpretation; honest labelling is an acceptable alternative. The gated O4/O5 still sample a rested, non-online operating point under 'no settle' wording, which would mislead. Major.
