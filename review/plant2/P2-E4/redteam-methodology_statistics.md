# P2-E4 draft red-team: methodology and statistics lens

Fresh reviewer given only the files (draft at commit 953851e); each finding then checked by a separate refuter. Run 2026-10-10. Recorded verbatim, before synthesis.

## Verdict

The draft cannot be frozen as written. Its M = 250 gated block cannot be scheduled under its own cohort-reservation rule. Once that is fixed, the main methodological problem is that the habituation gates cannot fail for the right reasons only. The habituation copies stop learning for 40 s, which is a settle: on seeds 42/43 the global vbar offset falls from 7.8 to 3.7 mV, and this hides habituation. O4 also confuses item unreliability with habituation: with zero habituation it fails with near certainty at the online recall rates seen in exploration. O0 is the only gated control and carries VOID, but it cannot fail: its targets never include the one item that has lingering activity, and its >= 0.8 bar would not trip even with no pre-gap. So F7's demand for a control that could come out the other way is still unmet. O3 at n = 40 fails about 30 % of the time from sampling alone even with no online cost. The power Monte Carlo models the wrong dependence. Exploration (a rough re-implementation, memory layer only) puts online recall at M = 1,000 at 0.70-0.81 against the 0.90 bar, so the likeliest verdict is ONLINE FAIL, not HABITUATION FAIL, and the verdict hierarchy would then hide the habituation result.

### M3 [major] O4 confuses item unreliability with habituation; HABITUATION FAIL can be reached with zero habituation

Name a failure HABITUATION FAIL only if the control passes that statistic and the repeated copy fails it, with a paired per-item test (McNemar) predeclared. ### M4 [major] O0, the only gated control and the one that carries VOID, cannot fail: wrong targets and an insensitive bar

### M6 [major] The power Monte Carlo models the wrong dependence, and its 5-repeat reliability arm is unspecified and biased

If a reliability arm is kept, specify sham encodings (fresh patterns, no plateau, no write) to hold the duty without adding load. Commit P(PASS) as an interval propagated from the rate's uncertainty, together with the probability of each criterion and of each verdict. Define 'the per-item rate that gives P(PASS) >= 0.8' as one common logit shift of all per-cue rates.

'Next list item' could mean the next learned item or the next of the 50 habituation items.
- recommendation: Predeclare the collateral item as the stored item whose assembly overlaps x's most, and report a random one as well. Score collateral as a paired difference between the repeated and control copies.
- independent verifier: **confirmed** (severity should be minor): The overlap arithmetic is right: |A| is about f_q x n = 0.005 x 4,000 = 20, so two random assemblies share about 20 x 20 / 4,000 = 0.1 cells. The collateral cue therefore reads mostly global operating-point change, which the no-learning copy pushes toward easier recall (M2). 'Next list item' (line 168) is ambiguous. ### M13 [minor] The no-probe twin changes the input duty as well as the probe history

- where: Draft lines 155-158 and 248
- evidence: With blank rolling slots, the twin's vbar runs lower before each block and then rises over about 20 steps (one tau) once the block's cues begin. Main minus no-probe therefore mixes write-history effects with an operating-point transient. Under the write oracle, probes can affect the stores only through later writes.
- recommendation: Fill the twin's rolling slots with novel cues (same input duty, no retrieval of stored items), or compare only the block's later steps. Report store-level differences (feedback-store digest, |R(x)|, Jaccard of R with A) as the primary reading.
- independent verifier: **partly** (severity should be minor): Holds: per line 156 the twin's rolling slots are blank, so its input duty differs, and main minus twin within a block mixes the store-level history with the current operating point. However, the draft's stated mechanism for probe-caused interference (lines 33-34: probes shift vbar, which shifts later responders and writes) makes the duty difference part of the treatment, not a pure confound. The vbar transient of about 20 steps at the start of each block is inferred from tau, not measured (no twin was run). Store-level readings (feedback-store size, |R(x)|) are already reported (lines 248-249). Filling the twin's rolling slots with novel cues to match duty is a good refinement for separating item-specific from duty effects.

### M14 [minor] Under the write oracle the probed against never-probed comparison is null by construction

- where: Draft lines 141-149 and 253
- evidence: Probes write nothing (a validity check confirms it). A probe's vbar trace decays with tau 10 s, so no item-specific trace of a probe can persist over the 230-780 s between a pool item's cohort probe and the M = 1,000 comparison; only diffuse effects through later writes remain. O3 still tests retention, but the reported probed against never-probed contrast can only show about zero.
- recommendation: Say so in the contract, and run the same contrast in the no-write-oracle stress arm, where probes can write and the comparison means something.
- independent verifier: **partly** (severity should be minor): For the memory part, the near-null result follows by construction. Probes never write, the forward store is BTSP on encoding input only, and a probe's vbar trace decays with tau 10 s. For the content part, 'null by construction' is overstated. A probe raises vbar on the probed item's assembly cells for some tau, which lowers their chance of joining R(z) for items encoded shortly afterwards and so permanently changes the feedback writes on those cells. That effect is item-specific, not diffuse, though small. The quoted gap of 230-780 s does not match the schedule: about 200-300 s from cohort-500 slots and 325-425 s from cohort-250 slots to the M = 1,000 cohort slots. The draft also never says when or how the probed cohorts are measured at M = 1,000; blocks contain no oldest-probed kind, so they are measured only in rolling steps up to 760. The contract should state that the contrast is expected to be near zero under the oracle and should define the measurement. Running the contrast in the no-write-oracle arm is sensible.

## Reviewer's predictions

These are my predictions if the draft were run as written, assuming M1 is patched minimally. They are extrapolated from the exploratory rough re-implementation on seeds 42/43, which simulated the memory layer only, so the content criteria are less certain.

- **O0:** passes on every seed and load with near certainty. Blank-target recall was 0 of 332 blank slots in exploration. The age-0 item is never a target, and its assembly is silent after 25 ms anyway. It is uninformative.
- **O1:** fails at M = 1,000 on essentially every seed. Recall >= 0.8 for recent and uniform cues was 0.70-0.81, against a 0.90 bar, because the online threshold offset is about 7-7.4 mV against about 3.7 mV settled. At M = 500 it is borderline: rates of 0.89-0.94 give P(pass on 5 seeds) of roughly 0.2-0.6. M = 250 passes. C2 and C3 pass (spurious 1.0, novel ignition 0-1 cells).
- **O2:** content tolerates partial memory recall (in P2-E3 the joint exceeded C1), so it should do better than O1. Even so, I expect a joint of about 0.80-0.93 at M = 1,000 and a failure on at least one seed. D3 passes.
- **O3:** fails at M = 1,000. Cohort cues sit at the highest offset of the block, and old items fared no better in exploration (ages 601-1,000: 0.56-0.71); n = 40 adds a sampling risk.
- **O4:** fails at both loads. Items with >= 8 of 10 late repetitions were 0.25-0.38 at M = 500 and 0.00 at M = 1,000, even in the draft's settling arm. An online variant habituates more.
- **O5:** the recovery cue reached recall >= 0.8 in 0.75-0.88 (M = 500) and 0.88-1.00 (M = 1,000) of the 8 items per seed in the draft arm, helped by the settle. I expect at least one seed-load to fail at n of about 40-47 scored items. The collateral cue is near certain to pass, since random assemblies barely overlap.
- **Verdict:** ONLINE FAIL, about 95 %; PASS, about 0 %. The habituation failure would then be hidden by the verdict hierarchy unless a verdict vector is recorded.
